## Problem 1 — Difficult to Keep Up with Constant Campus Announcements

**Problem:**  
Students often miss university events or updates because information is scattered across multiple Telegram channels and notice boards. They rely on word of mouth or friends to stay informed.

**When / Where it Happens:**  
Anytime important updates or club announcements are shared across too many channels.

**Who Experiences It:**  
KIU students who want to stay active in campus life but don’t know where to look for consistent information.

**Why It Matters:**  
It causes minor frustration and reduces participation in student events, but students usually adapt by asking friends — so the pain is moderate.

---

## Problem 2 — Students Feel Lost Balancing Academic Life and Mental Well-Being (Final Chosen Direction)

**Problem:**  
Many KIU students struggle to manage academic stress, loneliness, and emotional pressure throughout the semester. The fast pace, combined with high expectations and limited time for rest, often leads to burnout. Talking about mental health is still seen as uncomfortable, leaving students feeling isolated.

**When / Where it Happens:**  
During midterms and finals, late-night study sessions, or weeks with overlapping deadlines and projects.

**Who Experiences It:**  
Any KIU student juggling heavy coursework, group projects, and personal responsibilities — especially those living away from home.

**Why It Matters:**  
Unmanaged stress directly affects motivation, concentration, and performance.  
A platform that allows students to track mood, connect with supportive peers, and access campus well-being resources could make emotional health part of daily student life — not an afterthought.

---

## Problem 3 — Hard to Find Study Groups for Specific Courses

**Problem:**  
Students sometimes want to study with others taking the same course but struggle to coordinate schedules or discover who’s available.

**When / Where it Happens:**  
During exam prep periods, when students realize studying together could help but it’s too late to organize.

**Who Experiences It:**  
Mainly students preparing for technical or math-heavy subjects.

**Why It Matters:**  
It’s inconvenient but not critical — most students manage on their own.  
A better study-group feature could be nice to have, but it wouldn’t solve a major emotional or academic pain point.

---

**Summary:**  
While students face various small coordination issues on campus, the most significant and impactful challenge is **maintaining mental well-being amid academic stress**.  
Team UTOPIA will focus on **Problem 2 — Helping KIU students balance academic life and mental health** to promote a healthier, more supportive campus environment.
