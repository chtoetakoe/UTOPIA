## Overview

**Problem We're Solving:**  
KIU students struggle to balance academic workload with mental well-being, leading to stress, burnout, sleep disruption, and reduced motivation—especially during midterms/finals and project weeks.

**Our ICP in One Sentence:**  
First– to third-year KIU students taking 5+ courses who frequently feel overwhelmed by overlapping deadlines and prefer informal, peer-based support over formal counseling.

---

## Demographic Specifics

### Student/Professional Status

- **Level/Year:** 1st–3rd year undergraduates
- **Majors:** Primarily CS, Mathematics, Management
- **Program:** Full-time students

**Why this specificity matters:**

- 1st–3rd years report frequent exam/project crunch cycles; they’re still building routines.
- Seniors often have fewer courses or thesis-style work with different stress patterns.

### Additional Demographics

- **Age Range:** 18–23
- **Living Situation:** Mix of dorm residents and commuters (both experience stress; dorm residents report late-night study + isolation)
- **Work Status:** Mostly full-time students; a minority with part-time jobs/internships (higher strain)
- **Tech Profile:** Mobile-first; Telegram/Discord/WhatsApp daily; comfortable with web apps

### Academic Context

- **Course Load:** 5–6 courses/semester
- **Assessment Pattern:** Frequent weekly homework + 2 project-heavy courses; midterms/finals clustered
- **Schedule:** Classes 9:00–18:00; study sessions 19:00–01:00; peak crunch near deadlines

---

## Behavioral Characteristics

### Relationship with the Problem

**Frequency:**

- Stress spikes 3–5 days/week during busy periods
- Sleep disruption 1–3 nights/week in peak weeks

**Duration:**

- Recurs each semester; intensifies around Week 6–8 (midterms) and Week 13–15 (finals)

### Current Behaviors

- **Coping:** Music, memes, scrolling, short walks, naps, caffeine, “push through” mentality
- **Social:** Rarely initiates deep conversations about stress; uses emoji/humor to mask it
- **Tools:** Notes apps for to-do, phone reminders, ad-hoc journaling; some try habit trackers and drop them
- **Support Seeking:** Friends/roommates > public groups > formal school services
- **Information Diet:** Mental health reels/shorts, but inconsistent follow-through

### Motivation & Pain Tolerance

- **Motivation:** Wants quick, stigma-free support that feels normal; values small daily wins
- **Pain Tolerance:** Medium-high—“I can manage”—until crunch periods when it spikes

### Tech Savviness

- **Comfort:** Signs up easily, uses mobile daily, expects instant value
- **Implications:**
  - Onboarding ≤ 2 minutes
  - Daily micro-interactions (30–90 seconds)
  - Zero heavy setup; no essays; gentle nudges, not nagging

---

## Context & Constraints

### When Does the Problem Occur?

- **Time of Day:** Late evening (21:00–01:00) most intense; afternoons before labs also stressful
- **Day of Week:** Sun–Thu (assignment cycles); Sun nights = peak anxiety
- **Time of Semester:** Weeks 5–8, 13–15 (midterms/finals, project deadlines)
- **Triggers:** Multiple deadlines, group friction, low sleep, exam uncertainty

### Where Does It Occur?

- **Physical:** Dorm rooms, library quiet zones, labs, cafeteria corners
- **Digital:** Telegram/Discord chats, LMS tabs open, YouTube/Spotify on in background

### What Are They Trying to Accomplish? (JTBD)

> **When** workload piles up and motivation dips, **students want** a quick way to check in with themselves, feel understood, and regain focus **so that** they can study effectively without burning out.

**Secondary JTBD:**

> **When** they feel alone with stress, **they want** relatable, low-pressure peer connection **so that** they feel supported without stigma.

### Constraints

- **Time:** 10–15 minute breaks max; won’t sustain long rituals
- **Social:** Stigma; doesn’t want to “look weak” or overshare publicly
- **Money:** Expects free or university-supported tools
- **Authority:** Can’t change course schedules; limited influence over group pace

---

## Primary Pain Point (1–2 sentences)

KIU undergraduates juggling 5+ courses experience recurring stress and motivation dips during midterms/finals and project weeks. They want a private, low-effort way to reflect, feel understood by peers, and regain balance—without formal counseling or heavy apps.

---

## Where to Find Them (Specific)

1. Library quiet floor, **19:00–23:00**, Mon–Thu (solo study, visible stress)
2. Dorm study lounges, **21:00–01:00**, especially Week 6–8 & 13–15
3. CS/Engineering lab sessions (e.g., K-102), **Wed/Fri afternoons**
4. Large required lectures (DB Systems, Software Eng), **arrive last 10 min**
5. **KIU CS/Engineering Telegram** groups (post a short screener)
6. **Discord servers** for KIU cohorts; channels for study/help
7. Cafeteria tables with laptops open, **13:00–14:30** (light recruiting)
8. Student services boards (flyers with QR screener)
9. Peer mentors / RA networks (warm intros)
10. Hackathon prep meetups / study jams (pre-event gatherings)

---

## Screener Questions (to verify ICP)

1. **“How many courses are you taking this semester?”**
   - ✅ 5 or more preferred
2. **“In the last 2 weeks, how often did you feel stressed about deadlines?”**
   - ✅ 3+ days/week or “most days”
3. **“When you feel overwhelmed, who do you usually turn to first?”**
   - ✅ Friends/peers or “no one / I keep it to myself”
4. **“Would you prefer quick, private check-ins or long counseling sessions?”**
   - ✅ Quick, private, peer-friendly

_If 3–4 answers match the ✅ profile → strong ICP fit._

## Notes & Implications for MVP

- **Micro, not macro:** 30–90s check-ins; 1–2 taps
- **Privacy first:** Anonymized peer reflections; opt-in small groups
- **Immediate value:** Tiny wins (gratitude, mood trend, “you’re not alone” moments)
- **Peer energy:** Lightweight prompts; student-written tips; curated “what helped me” cards
- **Stigma-aware:** No clinical tone; friendly, student voice
- **Mobile-first:** Works offline moments; gentle notifications (not spam)
