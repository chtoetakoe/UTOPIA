## Problem Deep Dive

### The Story: What Happened

The interviewee described the week leading up to his Data Structures midterm. He had that exam on a Friday, a project for another course due the same day, and a programming assignment due on Wednesday. He started the week feeling motivated, planning to work on the project Monday and Tuesday, study for the exam Wednesday and Thursday, and finish the assignment in between.

By Tuesday night, the project was taking longer than expected. He stayed up until 3 AM working on it, sacrificing sleep. On Wednesday, he was too tired to focus on studying for the exam and spent hours debugging the assignment, feeling increasingly anxious. He skipped a club meeting to catch up. When a friend asked how he was, he just said, "Swamped, you know how it is," avoiding any real conversation. By Thursday, he was in a state of "pure panic," cramming for the exam on caffeine and minimal sleep. He submitted the project with a feature missing and felt unprepared for the midterm. He felt isolated and "stupid" for not managing his time better.

### Five Whys / Root Cause Exploration

**Surface problem:** He felt burned out and performed poorly despite working constantly.

**Why #1:** Why did you feel burned out?
**A:** “I didn't sleep for three nights straight, I was just coding and studying.”

**Why #2:** Why did you sacrifice sleep?
**A:** “It felt like the only way to get everything done. There weren’t enough hours in the day.”

**Why #3:** Why did you feel you had to do it all alone?
**A:** “I didn't want to complain to my friends. Everyone’s busy. It feels like a weakness.”

**Why #4:** Why does it feel like a weakness?
**A:** “Because at KIU, you’re supposed to be smart enough to handle the workload. Asking for help feels like admitting you can’t keep up.”

**Root Cause Assessment:**
The root cause is an internalised pressure to appear academically successful and self-reliant, which creates a social stigma around admitting stress, leading to isolation and unhealthy coping mechanisms like sacrificing sleep.

## Current Solutions & Workarounds

- **Primary Solution:** A combination of caffeine, loud music to focus, and a "brute force" mentality of working until the tasks are done.
- **Tools:** Uses a basic to-do list in a notes app but admits he often ignores it when he gets overwhelmed.
- **Alternatives Tried:** He tried a meditation app once but found it "cringey" and felt he "didn't have 10 minutes to just sit there."

---

## Key Insights & Learnings

1.  **Stigma is a powerful blocker.** The fear of looking "weak" or "incapable" is a bigger barrier to seeking support than the lack of resources.
2.  **Coping mechanisms are counterproductive.** Students are actively choosing strategies (sacrificing sleep) that harm their long-term performance to meet short-term goals.
3.  **Solutions must be fast and private.** The interviewee's rejection of a 10-minute meditation app shows that any proposed solution must demand minimal time and must not feel like another "task."
