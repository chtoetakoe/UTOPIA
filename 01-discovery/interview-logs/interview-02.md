## Problem Deep Dive

### The Story: What Happened

The interviewee described a recent Tuesday night. She had a math assignment due the next day that she was struggling with, and a reading for another class she hadn't started. She came back to her dorm after dinner feeling tired and isolated. Her roommate was out with friends. She sat down at her desk around 8 PM, opened her math homework, and couldn't solve the first problem. She immediately felt a wave of "panic and frustration."

Instead of trying a different problem or starting the reading, she closed her books, picked up her phone, and started scrolling through Instagram. She told herself it would be a "5-minute break," but ended up scrolling for over an hour. All she saw were posts of other students at events or out having fun, which made her feel even more lonely and behind. Around 10 PM, she felt guilty and tried to work on the math again, but couldn't focus. She ended up submitting the homework half-finished and didn't do the reading at all. She went to bed feeling like a failure.

### Five Whys / Root Cause Exploration

**Surface problem:** She procrastinated on her homework by scrolling on her phone.

**Why #1:** Why did you choose to scroll on your phone instead of working?
**A:** “Because the work felt too hard, and I just wanted to escape for a minute.”

**Why #2:** Why did the work feel so hard at that moment?
**A:** “I was already tired, and I felt really alone. It's hard to be motivated when you're in a bad mood.”

**Why #3:** Why were you in a bad mood?
**A:** “My roommate was out, and I felt like everyone else had friends and I was just stuck in my room. It made me feel like I didn't belong here.”

**Why #4:** Why does feeling like you don’t belong affect your homework?
**A:** “Because it makes everything feel pointless. Like, why am I even trying so hard if I'm miserable?”

**Root Cause Assessment:**
The root cause is not poor time management, but rather that feelings of social isolation and a lack of belonging create an emotional state that makes it nearly impossible to engage with challenging academic work.

## Current Solutions & Workarounds

- **Primary Solution:** Distraction through social media or YouTube. She calls it her "numbing" technique.
- **Secondary Solution:** Calling her parents, but she avoids this sometimes because she doesn't want to worry them.
- **Tools:** No specific tools for well-being. She uses Google Calendar for deadlines but doesn't find it helps with motivation.

---

## Key Insights & Learnings

1.  **Social connection is a prerequisite for academic resilience.** For some students, especially new ones, loneliness is the primary problem that makes academic stress feel insurmountable.
2.  **The problem isn't a lack of tools, but a lack of motivation.** The interviewee has calendars and to-do lists; what she lacks is the emotional energy to use them when feeling low.
3.  **Peer validation is the desired solution.** She isn't asking for a productivity tool; she's asking for a way to feel understood and connected to peers who are going through the same thing.
