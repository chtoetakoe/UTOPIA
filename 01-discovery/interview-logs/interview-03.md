## Problem Deep Dive

### The Story: What Happened

The interviewee described last Thursday. He had a lab report due at 5 PM and a quiz in his 9 AM class. He woke up at 6 AM to catch the 7 AM bus to have enough time to study on campus beforehand. The bus was late, and he got to campus with only 30 minutes before his quiz, feeling stressed and unprepared. After the quiz, he had a three-hour gap until his next class at 1 PM. He planned to use this time to finish his lab report.

He went to the library, but it was completely full. He walked to the CS building study lounge, which was also packed and noisy. He ended up finding an empty table in the corner of the cafeteria. By the time he got his laptop out, his battery was at 40%, and there were no open outlets. He spent the next two hours trying to work while anxiously watching his battery drain, getting constantly distracted by the noise. He had to leave at 12:40 PM to get to his next class. He didn't finish the report and had to rush to complete it after getting home at 7 PM, feeling exhausted and defeated.

### Five Whys / Root Cause Exploration

**Surface problem:** He struggled to complete his lab report on campus.

**Why #1:** Why was it a struggle?
**A:** “I couldn’t find a quiet place to work with a power outlet. The library was full.”

**Why #2:** Why was that so stressful?
**A:** “Because as a commuter, my time on campus is limited and precious. I can’t just go back to my room. Wasting two hours searching for a spot felt like a huge failure.”

**Why #3:** Why does that feeling of failure matter so much?
**A:** “Because it makes me feel like I’m at a disadvantage. Like the university isn't built for students like me. It's isolating.”

**Why #4:** Why does that feeling of isolation impact your work?
**A:** “It kills your motivation. You feel like you’re fighting all these extra battles just to do the same work as everyone else.”

**Root Cause Assessment:**
The root cause is a feeling of systemic disadvantage and isolation stemming from the university's infrastructure and social rhythms being misaligned with the logistical realities of a commuter student's life. This creates a constant, low-level stress that depletes the mental energy needed for academic tasks.

## Current Solutions & Workarounds

- **Primary Solution:** He has a "mental map" of backup study spots and carries a large power bank with him at all times.
- **Socially:** He relies on texting a few friends who are also commuters to share frustrations, but rarely talks to on-campus students about these issues.
- **He has given up on:** Attending evening events or joining clubs that meet after 6 PM.

---

## Key Insights & Learnings

1.  **Context is King.** The same academic workload creates a vastly different stress experience for a commuter versus an on-campus student.
2.  **The problem is environmental and logistical, not just internal.** For this segment, stress isn't just a feeling; it's a series of physical and logistical hurdles (finding a seat, power, quiet).
3.  **Lack of access to informal support is a major pain point.** He explicitly identified missing out on casual peer interactions as a source of both academic and social difficulty.
