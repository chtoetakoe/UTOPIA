## Problem Deep Dive

### The Story: What Happened

Student-Delta described her first semester after arriving three weeks late. By then, friend groups were formed and she felt “like a guest in someone else’s party.” She studied alone in the library and ate in her dorm room. Initially, she thought she’d adjust, but homesickness hit hard around midterms. She stopped going to study sessions and crammed alone until 3 a.m. Most days she woke up tired, missed breakfast, and barely focused in class. Grades dropped that semester. She said, “I felt like everyone was ahead and I was just trying to survive.”

Over time, she met two friends through a group project and that helped a bit, but she still feels that “deep connection is rare here.” Now in third year, she manages stress better but still dreads weeks when multiple deadlines collide because “it all comes back — the feeling of falling behind again.”

### Five Whys / Root Cause Exploration

**Surface problem:** Loneliness and academic burnout.  
**Why #1:** Why so lonely and drained? → “Came late, felt outsider, had no one to talk to.”  
**Why #2:** Why no one to talk to? → “Everyone already had friends and I didn’t want to bother them.”  
**Why #3:** Why afraid to bother them? → “Didn’t want to look needy or awkward.”  
**Why #4:** Why avoid reaching out even when it hurt? → “Didn’t think people would understand.”  
**Root cause:** Combination of late arrival, social anxiety, and lack of structured ways to meet peers or discuss mental well-being openly.

### Emotional Impact

- **Feelings:** Isolated, anxious, guilty about poor grades.
- **Words used:** “Left out,” “stuck,” “invisible.”
- **Body language/tone:** Soft voice, teary when recounting first year.
- **Life impact:** Lower grades, sleep trouble, and mild social withdrawal.

### Frequency & Pattern

- Happens every semester but spikes around exam weeks.
- Triggers: deadline clusters and social events she feels excluded from.
- Frequency has decreased slightly as she built two close friendships.
- Other instances: similar pattern before DB midterm this year – “stressed and silent for days.”

## Key Insights & Learnings

### Surprising Findings

- Early social isolation creates long-term academic anxiety even after social situation improves.
- Students rarely seek formal help — they normalize loneliness as “just part of college.”

### Contradictions or Tensions

- She claims to be “fine now” but admits stress returns each exam season.
- Wants connection but avoids events that could provide it.

### Patterns Emerging

- Mirrors Interview #001: stress + sleep problems around midterms.
- Emotional suppression common (“don’t want to worry others”).
- Reliance on Instagram and Messenger for comfort but no real support.

### Questions Raised

- Could peer-matching by shared struggles reduce loneliness?
- How might we encourage first-year integration for late arrivals?
- What would make seeking help feel normal instead of awkward?

### Confidence Level

☑ Somewhat confident — emotional pain is real and recurring, but more evidence needed on how students would engage with support tools.
