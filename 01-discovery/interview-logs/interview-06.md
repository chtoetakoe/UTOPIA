## Problem Deep Dive

### The Story: What Happened
Student-Zeta described failing both FOP and I2I during her first semester. The shock left her sleepless for days. “I kept replaying the grades page again and again,” she said. Her parents believed she was doing well, and she didn’t want to disappoint them.  

The fear of expulsion haunted her — if she failed the second time, she’d have to leave KIU. That thought “never left her mind” throughout the retake semester. She studied excessively, isolating herself from friends, surviving on coffee and anxiety. “I felt like my brain was constantly on fire,” she said.  

When she finally passed, she felt relief rather than pride. Even now, as a fourth-year student, she still feels anxiety spikes before exams. She believes many CS students experience similar silent breakdowns because “no one wants to look weak.”  

She wishes KIU provided more informal peer spaces to talk about academic fears — “something small and private, not a counselling office that feels too official.”

---

### Five Whys / Root Cause Exploration
*Surface problem:* Severe anxiety and isolation after course failure.  
*Why #1:* Why did failure hit so hard? → “Because I thought it meant I wasn’t capable of being here.”  
*Why #2:* Why did she believe that? → “Everyone around me seemed fine; I felt like the only one struggling.”  
*Why #3:* Why didn’t she share this with others? → “People don’t talk about failure here — it feels shameful.”  
*Why #4:* Why is failure shameful? → “We’re taught to compete, not admit weakness.”  
*Root cause:* Cultural and social stigma around failure leading to silence and burnout.
