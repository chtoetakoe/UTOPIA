Problem Deep Dive
The Story: What Happened
Student-Eta walked me through a typical morning. Alarm goes off at 7 AM. He rushes to get ready, grabs whatever quick breakfast he can find, and leaves by 8:10 to catch the marshrutka. "Some mornings the marshrutka is packed and I have to wait for the next one," he explained. "That means I'm late, and professors notice."
He recalled one particularly bad morning during midterms when he missed the marshrutka completely and had to take a taxi. "I spent 15 lari just to not miss an exam. That's like three days of lunch money."
Living at home wasn't his choice. When he started at KIU, G and F dorms were already full, and his family lives in Kutaisi anyway. "I thought it would be fine," he said. "But by third year, watching my friends hang out after class, going to study sessions at 8 PM, staying up coding together… I just go home and crash."
Now in 7th semester, every assignment feels critical. "I check Moodle constantly," he admitted. "Even when I know nothing's posted yet. It's like I'm waiting for something to go wrong." He's passing everything currently, but the fear of failure is paralyzing. "If I fail one course, I don't graduate. My parents have already planned around me graduating this year."
The isolation hits hardest during group projects. "Everyone wants to meet at the dorms or study late on campus, but I have to catch the last marshrutka by 10. So I end up doing my part alone at home, and I feel disconnected from my team."
When I asked what would help, he immediately said: "Honestly? Just don't schedule core CS courses at 9 AM. Or give us some online options so I don't have to physically be there for everything. I'm exhausted before the semester even starts."

Five Whys / Root Cause Exploration
Surface problem: Severe exhaustion and anxiety from commuting to early lectures.
Why #1: Why is commuting so draining? → "Because I lose 2-3 hours every day just traveling, and I'm already tired before class starts."
Why #2: Why does that create such anxiety? → "Because if I'm late or miss attendance, it affects my grade, and I can't afford any mistakes."
Why #3: Why couldn't he get dorm housing? → "Dorms were full when I applied, and even now there's a waiting list."
Why #4: Why does this feel unbearable specifically now? → "Because this is my last semester — if I fail anything, I have to stay another whole year."
Root cause: Limited housing capacity and inflexible early-morning scheduling create structural disadvantages for commuting students, amplified by high-stakes graduation requirements.

Housing scarcity creates educational inequality that compounds over years.
