## Problem Deep Dive

**The Story: What Happened**
Student-Theta excelled in Math but learned in second year that her degree requires several mandatory CS courses. She found the Introduction to Programming overwhelming, feeling that others had coded before while she was starting with the very basics. Now in third year, courses like Data Structures and Algorithms feel **impossible**. She notes that in Math, she can eventually understand a concept, but in CS, she feels like she's "just **memorizing syntax** and hoping it works."

She spends **10+ hours** on CS assignments that her CS-major friends finish in 30 minutes. The worst part is the impact on her overall motivation: "I used to love studying. Now I dread opening my laptop." She's started skipping CS lectures because sitting there confused makes her feel "**useless**." Her Math grades are strong, but the CS courses are dragging down her GPA, and she fears failing and having to retake a course. She's accepted she needs to simply "**survive** these courses and get back to actual Math."

Sharing that the interviewer (a Management student) also struggles with required CS courses provided immediate relief: “Oh my God, you get it. Management students have to take CS too?” She agreed, “It's like we're being set up to fail.”

## Five Whys / Root Cause Exploration

- **Surface problem:** Severe stress and loss of motivation from struggling in required CS courses.
- **Why #1:** Why do CS courses cause such stress? → "Because I don't understand the material even when I try really hard."
- **Why #2:** Why is the material so hard to understand? → "Because it's taught assuming we already know basics, and I don't have any CS background."
- **Why #3:** Why doesn't she seek help? → "I do, but office hours are always full of CS majors asking advanced questions. I'm too embarrassed to ask basic things."
- **Why #4:** Why does this affect her Math performance too? → "Because CS homework takes all my time and energy, and I lose motivation for everything."
- **Root cause:** Required cross-disciplinary courses without adequate foundational support or differentiated instruction create disproportionate burden for students outside the primary major, leading to burnout and academic identity crisis.

## Key Insights & Learnings

### Surprising Findings
- Students in **non-CS majors** face severe struggles with required CS courses but lack appropriate support systems.
- Loss of motivation in difficult required courses **spreads** to subjects where student previously excelled.
- **Office hours** and existing help systems feel inaccessible to students who need the most basic support.
- Shared experience with interviewer immediately reduced shame and increased openness — suggests **peer support** could be powerful.

### Contradictions or Tensions
- Wants good grades but has lowered expectations to just "**surviving**."
- Values understanding but resorts to copying code without comprehension.
- Needs help but too embarrassed to seek it in official channels.
- Loves learning (Math) but dreads studying (CS).

### Patterns Emerging
- Echoes Interview #006 (Zeta): fear of failure and shame preventing help-seeking.
- Echoes Interview #007 (Eta): high-stakes pressure to pass all courses.
- **New dimension:** required cross-major courses create unique burnout when students lack prerequisites or aptitude.
- Confirms pattern: students **hide struggles** and feel isolated even when problem is widespread.
