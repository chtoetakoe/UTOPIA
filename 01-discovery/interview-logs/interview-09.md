## Problem Deep Dive

**The Story: What Happened**
Student-Iota chose CS based on **external advice** (teachers, parents) about practicality and demand. She wasn't passionate, but she thought it was the "smart choice." First year was manageable, but she noticed her peers were coding for *fun*, a feeling she didn't share. Second year, the work feels "**mechanical. Empty**."

She tried exploring minors to spark interest. **Math** was too abstract and made her feel "stupid." **Management** was too focused on group projects and presentations, which she disliked. She is now "**stuck in pure CS**," avoiding the decision to choose a minor or plan her career.

The uncertainty is compounded by family pressure; her parents "panic" when she hints at switching, constantly reinforcing that "**CS is the future**." She feels she might be throwing away her future by staying in something she doesn't love, but the **existential dread** of not knowing what she wants is easier to avoid by just focusing on passing current courses.

---

## Five Whys / Root Cause Exploration

- **Surface problem:** Feeling lost and unmotivated in CS major, uncertain about minor selection or career direction.
- **Why #1:** Why does CS feel wrong? → "Because I don't feel passionate about it. It's just... work."
- **Why #2:** Why did she choose CS in the first place? → "Because everyone said it was practical and smart. I didn't have a better idea."
- **Why #3:** Why hasn't she switched majors? → "Because I don't know what I'd switch to. And my parents would be upset."
- **Why #4:** Why can't she figure out what she wants? → "Because I've never really asked myself what I actually like. I've just been following advice."
- **Root cause:** Choosing major based on **external expectations and practicality** rather than self-knowledge and interest, combined with lack of structured self-exploration opportunities and **fear of disappointing others**, leads to existential burnout and paralysis.

## Key Insights & Learnings

### Surprising Findings
- Academic burnout can stem from **existential uncertainty** and **lack of purpose**, distinct from workload.
- Students choose majors based on **external validation** (job market, parental pressure) without adequate self-exploration.
- Fear of disappointing others prevents honest career introspection.
- Many students are "**successfully failing**" — passing courses but deeply dissatisfied.

### Contradictions or Tensions
- Performs adequately in CS but feels no connection to it.
- Wants to find passion but avoids self-exploration.
- Values job security (external) but craves meaning (internal).
