# Interview Script v1 – Academic Balance & Mental Well-Being

**Team:** UTOPIA  
**Problem Focus:** KIU students struggle to maintain balance between academic workload and mental well-being  
**Target ICP:** First–third year KIU students taking 5+ courses, experiencing recurring stress and burnout during midterms and finals  
**Script Version:** 1.0

---

## Pre-Interview Checklist

Before starting:

- [ ] Confirmed interviewee is a KIU student (1st–3rd year)
- [ ] Confirmed they’re taking 5+ courses this semester
- [ ] Found a quiet space (20–25 minutes)
- [ ] Asked for permission to record or take notes
- [ ] Reviewed script but will speak naturally, not robotically

---

## SECTION 1: Opening & Rapport (3–5 minutes)

**Opening:**  
“Hi [name], thanks for taking a few minutes to talk with me! I really appreciate it.”

**Introduction:**  
“I’m [your name], a student at KIU. I’m working on a course project for _Product Development for Software Engineers_, and we’re exploring how students handle academic pressure, balance their schedules, and manage their mental well-being.”

“I’m not here to offer any advice or promote anything — I just want to understand what your real experience has been like this semester. There are no right or wrong answers — we’re just trying to learn.”

**Logistics:**  
“This will take around 20–25 minutes. Is that okay for you?”  
[Wait for confirmation]

“Would it be okay if I record the conversation? It’s only for our team to take accurate notes — we won’t share it.”  
[Wait for confirmation]

“Awesome, let’s start! Feel free to be as open as you like — this is fully anonymous and casual.”

---

## SECTION 2: Context & Background (5–7 minutes)

**Q1:** “What year are you currently in at KIU?”

**Q2:** “How many courses are you taking this semester?”

**Q3:** “When do you usually feel the busiest or most stressed during the semester?”

**Q4:** “Can you describe what a typical study day looks like for you — from morning to night?”

**Q5:** “Where do you usually study — dorms, library, labs, somewhere else?”

**Q6:** “When you have several assignments or deadlines close together, how do you usually plan your time?”

**Q7:** “Who do you usually talk to when you’re feeling overwhelmed — friends, family, no one?”

---

## SECTION 3: Problem Deep Dive (10–15 minutes) MOST IMPORTANT

**MAIN QUESTION:**  
“Think about the last time you felt really overwhelmed or burned out because of university work. Can you walk me through exactly what happened — from the moment you realized you were stressed to how you dealt with it?”

[Pause. Let them tell their story in detail. Use soft follow-ups only when needed.]

---

### Follow-Up Probes (use naturally, not all at once)

**Digging Deeper:**

- “You mentioned [specific thing they said, e.g., staying up late]. Tell me more about that — what made you do that?”
- “What usually causes that kind of stress for you?”
- “How often does that happen — every week, every few weeks?”

**Five Whys (Root Cause Exploration):**

1. “Why do you think that situation felt so stressful?”
2. “Why do you think it keeps happening?”
3. “Why does that matter to you personally?”
4. “Why do you think it’s hard to change it?”
5. “Why do you think most students around you experience the same thing?”

**Emotional Impact:**

- “How did that make you feel at the time?”
- “Did it affect your sleep, motivation, or mood?”
- “When that happens, what usually goes through your head?”

**Consequences:**

- “Did that stress affect how well you studied or performed in class?”
- “Did it affect your relationships or social life?”
- “How long did it take to feel back to normal?”

**Patterns:**

- “Is this kind of stress common for you, or was that week unusual?”
- “What’s the difference between a good week and a bad week for you?”

---

## SECTION 4: Current Solutions & Coping Methods (3–5 minutes)

**Q1:** “When you’re under a lot of pressure, what do you usually do to cope or calm down?”

**Q2:** “What do you think helps the most — and what doesn’t really work for you?”

**Q3:** “Have you ever tried using any apps or techniques for managing stress or mental health — like journaling, meditation, or habit trackers?”

- “If yes, tell me about the last time you used one. What made you stop or continue?”
- “If no, have you ever thought about it? What’s held you back?”

**Q4:** “Do you think KIU provides enough support for students’ mental well-being? Why or why not?”

**Q5:** “If you could design something small that genuinely helped students feel less stressed or more balanced, what would it look like?”

---

## SECTION 5: Closing & Next Steps (2–3 minutes)

**Gratitude:**  
“Thank you so much for sharing all of that — this was really helpful. I know it’s not always easy to talk about stress, so I really appreciate your honesty.”

**Referrals:**  
“Do you know any other students — maybe your classmates or dorm mates — who might also want to share their experiences with academic stress? We’re looking for a few more people to talk to.”

[If yes] “Would you be comfortable introducing me, or should I just mention that you suggested it?”

**Future Contact:**  
“If we later build something to help students manage stress and balance studies, would you be interested in trying it out?”

[If yes]  
“Great! What’s the best way to reach you?”

- Email: [_________]
- Telegram/WhatsApp: [_________]

**Final Thank You:**  
“Thanks again! I really appreciate your time and honesty — hope you have a lighter week ahead.”

## Notes for Interviewer

**Good signs:**

- Interviewee shares specific stories (“Last Tuesday when I had two deadlines…”)
- Mentions emotions or coping behaviors
- Describes what worked and what didn’t

**Red flags:**

- Hypothetical answers (“If I were stressed, I would…”) → redirect to real example
- Generic answers (“I just study”) → ask for _last time it happened_

**Reminders:**

- Be quiet — let silence work
- Don’t pitch solutions
- Follow curiosity, not checklist
