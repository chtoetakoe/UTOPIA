## Overview

This document captures the affinity mapping exercise where we transformed raw interview data into clustered themes and patterns.

**Methodology:**
- Extracted 68 key insights from 10 interview logs.
- Created digital sticky notes in Miro with individual insights (quotes, pain points, behaviors).
- Grouped similar insights into clusters through silent sorting.
- Identified 6 distinct themes that define the problem space.

## Clusters Identified

### Cluster 1: Fear of Judgment (Stigma)

**Frequency:** 9/10 interviews mentioned this.
**Sample Insights:**
1.  "You can't be the one who admits you're falling behind." (Interview #01)
2.  "I don't want to worry my parents, so I just say everything's fine." (Interview #02)
3.  "Everyone else looks like they have it together. Complaining just feels like making excuses." (Interview #05)
4.  "It feels like a weakness... you're supposed to be smart enough to handle the workload." (Interview #01)

**Initial Pattern Observation:** There is a powerful social pressure to project an image of success and competence. Admitting to stress is perceived as a personal failure, which leads to students hiding their struggles.

---

### Cluster 2: Feeling Alone (Isolation)

**Frequency:** 8/10 interviews mentioned this.
**Sample Insights:**
1.  "It's doing it alone in my room at 11 PM and feeling like I'm the only person in the world who doesn't get it." (Interview #02)
2.  "You miss out on that informal support, the random conversations where you realize you're not the only one struggling." (Interview #03, Commuter)
3.  "My friends here are great, but we only talk about classes or parties. We don't really talk about... this stuff." (Interview #04)
4.  "I see everyone's fun stories on Instagram, and I'm just stuck in my room. It makes it worse." (Interview #02)

**Initial Pattern Observation:** Academic stress is intensely magnified by social and emotional isolation. The feeling of being "the only one" is a core, recurring pain point.

---

### Cluster 3: Unhealthy Coping is the Default (Workarounds)

**Frequency:** 8/10 interviews mentioned this.
**Sample Insights:**
1.  "I just drink another coffee and tell myself I can sleep after the deadline." (Interview #01)
2.  "Sometimes I have so much to do that I just... don't do anything. I just close it and scroll on my phone for an hour." (Interview #02)
3.  "My 'break' is just switching from my coding IDE to YouTube. It doesn't actually help me relax." (Interview #07)
4.  "I just brute force it. It's not healthy, but it's the only way." (Interview #05)

**Initial Pattern Observation:** Students lack a toolkit of healthy, low-effort coping strategies. Their default behaviors are either avoidance (distraction) or unhealthy persistence (sacrificing health), which ultimately worsens the problem.

---

### Cluster 4: The Problem is Physical & Logistical (Context)

**Frequency:** 4/10 interviews mentioned this (all commuters + 1 on-campus).
**Sample Insights:**
1.  "For me, stress isn't just about the work. It's about 'Will I miss this bus?'... It all just stacks up." (Interview #03)
2.  "I can't just go back to my dorm to take a 30-minute nap. My 'breaks' are spent walking to the next building." (Interview #06, Commuter)
3.  "I feel like the university isn't built for students like me. It's isolating." (Interview #03)

**Initial Pattern Observation:** For a significant segment (commuters), the root of stress is environmental and logistical. Their mental energy is drained by external factors before academic work even begins.

---

### Cluster 5: Existing Solutions Don't Stick

**Frequency:** 6/10 interviews mentioned this.
**Sample Insights:**
1.  "We tried using Trello once, but it felt like extra work and no one kept it updated." (Sample Log)
2.  "I tried a meditation app... felt I didn't have 10 minutes to just sit there." (Interview #01)
3.  "I downloaded a habit tracker for 'self-care' but after three days of missing entries, it just became another source of guilt." (Interview #08)
4.  "I don't need a counselor to tell me to 'breathe.' I just want to know that other people also feel like a mess." (Interview #02)

**Initial Pattern Observation:** Formal or high-effort solutions are consistently rejected. They are perceived as either too time-consuming, too clinical, or become another source of pressure when users fail to maintain them.

---

### Cluster 6: The Post-Burnout Crash

**Frequency:** 7/10 interviews mentioned this.
**Sample Insights:**
1.  "After my last midterm, I slept for 14 hours and still felt exhausted. I couldn't focus for two days." (Interview #01)
2.  "The worst part is the week after finals. You have no motivation to do anything, not even fun stuff." (Interview #04)
3.  "I passed the exam, but I didn't actually learn anything." (Interview #01)

**Initial Pattern Observation:** The cycle of high stress doesn't end when a deadline passes. It's followed by a significant "crash" period characterized by exhaustion and lack of focus, which impacts both well-being and learning retention.
