# Final Evidence-Based Problem Statement

**Team Name:** UTOPIA
**Date:** 2025-10-31
**Based on:** 10 customer discovery interviews (Weeks 3-4)

---

## Executive Summary

**One-Sentence Problem Statement:**
First to third-year KIU students with heavy course loads struggle to manage the emotional toll of academic pressure because a culture of perceived competence and a lack of informal peer connection creates intense feelings of isolation, leading them to adopt unhealthy coping mechanisms that result in burnout.

---

## Problem Statement Components

### Component 1: Specific Segment (WHO)

**Validated ICP:** First to third-year KIU students taking 5+ courses who feel overwhelmed during peak academic periods. We've validated two important sub-segments with distinct environmental factors but shared emotional pain: **on-campus residents** (struggle with late-night loneliness) and **commuters** (struggle with logistical stress and disconnection from campus social life).
*Evidence: 9/10 interviews matched this profile.*

---

### Component 2: Specific Problem (WHAT)

**The Problem:** Students struggle to manage the **emotional toll of academic pressure**, which manifests as an intense feeling of being alone in their struggle.
*Evidence: The "Feeling Alone (Isolation)" pattern was present in 8/10 interviews, with interviewees using words like "alone in my room," "only person in the world," and "miss out on support."*

---

### Component 3: Context (WHEN/WHERE)

**Context:** The problem is most acute during high-stakes academic periods (midterms, finals), often when students are studying alone in the evenings (21:00-01:00) in their dorm rooms or at the library.
*Evidence: 8/10 interviewees described a specific story of being overwhelmed that took place late at night while they were by themselves.*

---

### Component 4: Root Cause (WHY)

**Root Cause:** A pervasive culture of perceived competence creates a **social stigma** around admitting stress, leaving no safe, informal channel for students to feel validated.
*Evidence: The "Fear of Judgment (Stigma)" pattern was the most frequent (9/10 interviews). The Five Whys analysis revealed that students avoid talking about stress because it feels like admitting a "weakness" in an environment that rewards high achievement.*

---

### Component 5: Measurable Consequences (IMPACT)

**Impact:** This leads to cycles of burnout and isolation, where students adopt unhealthy coping mechanisms (mentioned by 8/10) like sacrificing sleep or stress-induced procrastination (mentioned by 7/10), which negatively affects both their mental health and academic performance (e.g., "I passed the exam, but I didn't actually learn anything" - Interview #01).
*Evidence: The "Post-Burnout Crash" was a pattern in 7/10 interviews.*

---

## FINAL PROBLEM STATEMENT

### Complete Statement

First to third-year KIU students with heavy course loads struggle to manage the emotional toll of academic pressure during high-stakes periods like midterms and finals. This struggle is magnified because a culture of perceived competence creates a strong social stigma around admitting vulnerability, leaving students feeling intensely isolated. As a result, they default to unhealthy coping mechanisms (like sacrificing sleep or avoidance), leading to recurring cycles of burnout that damage both their mental well-being and their ability to learn effectively.

**Evidence:** *Validated across 10 customer interviews. Key patterns included "Fear of Judgment (Stigma)" (9/10 interviews), "Feeling Alone (Isolation)" (8/10), and "Unhealthy Coping Mechanisms" (8/10). The root cause was identified as a lack of safe, informal channels for peer validation in a high-pressure environment.*

---

## Problem Statement Evolution

### Week 2 Hypothesis (Original)

"KIU undergraduates juggling 5+ courses experience recurring stress and motivation dips... They want a private, low-effort way to reflect, feel understood by peers, and regain balance..."

### Week 4 Validated Statement (Evidence-Based)

The final statement above, which shifts the focus from "reflecting" to tackling the core drivers: **stigma and isolation**.

### What Changed & Why

The core problem of "stress" was correct, but our initial "Why" was a solution hypothesis ("they want a way to reflect"). The interviews taught us the true "Why" is the cultural stigma and resulting isolation. We moved from describing a desire to defining the fundamental barrier. We learned that **connection and validation** are the Jobs To Be Done, not just introspection.
