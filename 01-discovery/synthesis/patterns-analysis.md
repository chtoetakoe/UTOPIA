### Pattern 1: Fear of Judgment (Stigma)

**Intensity Rating:** ⚠️⚠️⚠️⚠️⚠️ (5/5 - Severe Pain)
**Emotional Language Used:** "Can't admit," "weakness," "feel stupid," "have to pretend."
**Evidence of Impact:** This is a root cause of isolation. It forces students into unhealthy coping mechanisms because they feel they can't ask for help or be vulnerable. It is the primary blocker to seeking any kind of support.
**Is this "hair on fire"?** ✅ Yes. It's not an acute pain, but a chronic, high-anxiety state that dictates all other behaviors.

### Pattern 2: Feeling Alone (Isolation)

**Intensity Rating:** ⚠️⚠️⚠️⚠️⚠️ (5/5 - Severe Pain)

### Pattern 3: Unhealthy Coping is the Default

**Intensity Rating:** ⚠️⚠️⚠️⚠️ (4/5 - Major Pain)

**Evidence of Impact:** Directly leads to the "Post-Burnout Crash." Students know it's not working ("I didn't actually learn anything") but feel they have no alternative. The pain here is the feeling of being trapped in a destructive cycle.

## Root Cause Analysis

### Primary Pattern: Students feel isolated and hide their stress.

**Five Whys Exercise:**

**Surface Problem:** Students feel stressed and alone during peak academic periods.

1.  **Why do they feel alone?**
    → Because everyone else seems to be handling the workload successfully, and no one is openly talking about struggling.

2.  **Why is no one talking about it?**
    → Because admitting stress feels like admitting a personal weakness or that you're not "smart enough" to be at KIU. (Stigma)

3.  **Why does it feel like a weakness?**
    → The environment implicitly rewards results (grades, project completion) over process or well-being. Success is defined by output, not by sustainable effort.

4.  **Why does this lead to isolation?**
    → Since everyone is projecting an image of competence, students internalize their struggles as unique personal failures, making them even less likely to reach out.

5.  **Why don't existing support systems fix this?**
    → Formal systems (counseling) are seen as too high-stakes and clinical, reinforcing the idea that your problem is "serious." Informal systems (friends) are often avoided due to the stigma.

**ROOT CAUSE IDENTIFIED:**
The academic culture values visible achievement over invisible well-being, creating a social stigma around discussing struggles. This leaves no safe, informal, low-stakes channel for students to feel validated and connected in their shared experience of stress.
