# Problem Statement Review

**Team:** UTOPIA
**Date:** 2025-10-21
**Interviews Completed:** 3 interviews
**Review Status:** **Refine**

---

## Original Problem Statement (from Lab 2)

KIU undergraduates juggling 5+ courses experience recurring stress and motivation dips during midterms/finals and project weeks. They want a private, low-effort way to reflect, feel understood by peers, and regain balance—without formal counseling or heavy apps.

-   **WHO:** KIU undergraduates (1st-3rd year) with 5+ courses.
-   **WHAT:** Experience stress and motivation dips.
-   **WHEN/WHERE:** During peak academic times (midterms/finals).
-   **WHY:** They desire a private, low-effort, peer-focused way to find balance.
-   **IMPACT:** Burnout, reduced motivation, poor performance.

---

## Key Interview Findings (So Far)

Based on 3 interviews completed:

1.  **Stigma is a primary blocker:** Students actively hide their stress to avoid looking weak or incapable (Interview #001).
2.  **Social isolation dramatically worsens academic stress:** The feeling of being "the only one" is a core pain point, especially for new students and commuters (Interview #002, #003).
3.  **Coping mechanisms are often counterproductive:** The default is either to "push through" by sacrificing health or "escape" via distraction, both of which increase long-term stress (Interview #001, #002).
4.  **Context matters:** The commuter experience introduces significant logistical stressors that are absent for on-campus students, changing the nature of the problem (Interview #003).

---

## Analysis: Validation or Challenge?

### What Interviews VALIDATED ✅

-   The core problem of stress and burnout during peak times is **very real and painful** for students (Validated in all 3 interviews).
-   Students **prefer informal support** and are hesitant to use formal counseling or "heavy" apps (Validated in #001, #002).
-   The pain leads to **negative academic and health consequences**, like poor focus and sleep loss (Validated in #001, #003).

### What Interviews CHALLENGED ❌

-   **The "Why" was incomplete.** We thought the problem was just about managing academic work. Interviews revealed the "why" is much deeper: it's driven by **social stigma** and **feelings of isolation**. The academic stress is often a symptom of these deeper social/emotional needs.
-   **The "Who" (ICP) is not uniform.** The experience of a commuter (Interview #003) is fundamentally different from an on-campus student. Lumping them together hides crucial pain points. The problem for commuters is environmental and logistical as much as it is academic.

---

## Decision: Keep, Refine, or Pivot?

**Our Decision:** ✅ **REFINE**

**Justification:**
The core problem of student well-being is strongly validated. However, our initial understanding was too shallow. We didn't grasp the critical role that social connection and stigma play. The problem isn't just "I'm stressed by my work," it's "I'm stressed by my work, and I feel alone in it, and I'm afraid to admit it." Furthermore, the commuter vs. on-campus context is a significant variable we need to account for. A pivot isn't necessary because the general problem space is correct, but a refinement is crucial to ensure we're solving the *right* part of the problem.

---

## Revised Problem Statement

### Refined Statement v2:

First to third-year KIU students with heavy course loads struggle to manage the emotional toll of academic pressure **because of social stigma and a lack of informal, low-friction peer connection**, leading to cycles of burnout and isolation. They need a private, stigma-free way to feel understood and validated by their peers, so they can maintain motivation and emotional balance during high-stress periods like midterms and finals.

### Comparison:

| Component | Original | Revised | Why Changed |
|:---------|:--------|:-------|:-----------|
| **WHO** | KIU undergraduates (1-3 yr) | (Same, but we now recognize sub-segments like commuters) | Interviews revealed important sub-groups. |
| **WHAT** | Stress & motivation dips | The **emotional toll** of academic pressure | Reframed to be more about the emotional experience, not just the academic tasks. |
| **WHY** | Want a private, low-effort way to reflect/balance | **Because of social stigma and lack of peer connection** | This is the biggest change. We identified a deeper root cause from the interviews. The need for connection is the driver. |
| **IMPACT** | Burnout, reduced motivation | Cycles of **burnout and isolation** | Added "isolation" as a key negative outcome, as validated by interviews. |

---

## Confidence Level

**Current confidence:** **Medium (70%)**

We are highly confident that stress, stigma, and isolation are real, painful problems. We are less confident about whether a single solution can address the nuanced differences between a first-year worried about loneliness and a third-year commuter worried about logistics.

**What would increase confidence?**
-   Interviewing more commuters to see if the patterns from Interview #003 hold.
-   Specifically probing on the "stigma" and "connection" themes with more students.
-   Understanding if students see these problems as connected or separate.

---

## Impact on Interview Strategy

### Changes to Script (for v2)

-   Add a question specifically about commuting: "How does getting to and from campus affect your study routine and stress levels?"
-   Dig deeper on social connection: "When you feel overwhelmed, how important is it to know that other students are feeling the same way? Why?"
-   Be more direct about stigma: "Do you ever feel like you can't talk about stress because you don't want to seem like you're not handling things? Tell me about that."
