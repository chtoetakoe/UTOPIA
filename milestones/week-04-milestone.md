## Problem Statement

### Final Evidence-Based Statement

> **First- to third-year KIU students with heavy course loads struggle to manage the emotional toll of academic pressure because a culture of perceived competence and a lack of informal peer connection create intense feelings of isolation. This leads to unhealthy coping behaviors that result in burnout and decreased academic engagement.**

**Evidence:** Validated across 10 interviews.

- _Fear of Judgment (Stigma):_ 9 / 10
- _Feeling Alone (Isolation):_ 8 / 10
- _Unhealthy Coping:_ 8 / 10

## Lessons Learned

**About Discovery:**

1. Emotional honesty emerges after trust is built.
2. Stigma is the invisible barrier behind most academic stress.
3. Open conversation is more valuable than quantitative surveys.

**About the Problem:**

1. Students don’t need another “productivity tool.”
2. They crave safe peer validation more than solutions.
3. Burnout recovery requires emotional connection, not just time.

**About the ICP:**

1. Commuters face higher isolation due to environment.
2. On-campus students experience loneliness at night, not in classes.

---

### Unexpected Findings

- Many students reject formal solutions (therapy, apps).
- “I just want to know I’m not the only one” appeared across multiple logs.
- The stigma of weakness fuels every other pattern.

**Contradiction:** Initial assumption was _workload stress_; true driver is _emotional isolation_.

### Design Phase Preparation

**Solution Constraints:**

1. Must be low-effort and mobile-accessible.
2. Must feel informal and safe (no stigma).
3. Should leverage existing communication channels (Telegram, Discord).

**Key User Needs:**

1. Validation from peers.
2. Freedom from fear of judgment.
3. Mental relief without added workload.

**Initial Solution Direction:**

> A lightweight, anonymous peer-support tool where KIU students can share short reflections or stress moments, read others’ experiences, and feel seen—reducing stigma and rebuilding connection.
